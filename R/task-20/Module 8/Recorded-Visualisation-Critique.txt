The data story I chose is: Limbo Lines: Dead Here, Alive There

** Question 1: What type of storytelling was used and/or what was the structure of the story? (Think about Ben Jones’s Seven Data Story Types or any other story types from this week’s materials.)

Referring to Ben Jones's seven data story types, the one used here is: "Highlight Contrasts". Indeed, this story highlights the differences in laws and guidelines between different regions of the world.

** Question 2: What was your main takeaway? 

From this story we can draw that depending on the laws and guidelines in different regions a brain dead person can be declared officially dead in one region but by moving him to another region would be declared alive. This state of brain death saves lives because the organs of these people can be transplanted to other patients in need. It should be noted that this organ transplantation is only possible over a certain period of time. This highlights our problem that depending on the region, a brain-dead person can be declared officially dead sooner and the organs used. 

** Question 3: Was the overall story effective? Why or why not?

The overall story is effective. In fact, I would like to point out that the writing format used is very similar to the Lead-Development-Resolution format that is used in newspaper articles. First the Lead which exposes the global state and puts us directly in front of the main problem, then the Development part which explains a little more the problem and its origin and finally the Resolution part which exposes the details and actions. The story did not allow us to be distracted, it attracted our curiosity from the first lines of the writing.
